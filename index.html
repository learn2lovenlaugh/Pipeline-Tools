The whole picture

PF against length, with your pressure drawn in

Solid red line is operating pressure — cross it and the pipe ruptures. Dashed line is operating ÷ F, the repair trigger. Where the 99% wall curve meets the red line is the crossover length.

How the crossover length is found

The tool grows the feature to 99% wall — effectively through — and solves for the length at which PF equals operating pressure. Shorter than that, the pipe perforates while still holding pressure: a leak. Longer, the failure pressure drops below operating before the wall is consumed: a rupture.

Treat the deep end as directional. B31G was fit to burst tests on features up to about 80% wall. Above that the equation still returns a number, but it is extrapolation. The crossover length is a screening indicator, not a design value.

Leak-before-rupture is not a property of short flaws alone. It also needs steel tough enough to arrest a running crack. Low-toughness vintage material — pre-1970 ERW, lap-welded, some seamless — can turn a pinhole into a rupture regardless of what this chart says.

Length rarely holds still. Two features within 6t merge into one, so a short feature becomes a long one by meeting a neighbor rather than by growing. A leak prediction assumes nothing new appears nearby.
